Title

Apparent Power Examples (Part B)

Introduction

  • Scope: Walk through three typical scenarios of apparent power S in AC circuits: phasor product, complex impedance, and transformer ratings.
  • Why it matters: Apparent power bridges voltage and current magnitudes regardless of phase, underpinning sizing of equipment and conductors.
  • What you’ll learn: How to compute S, separate it into P and Q when impedance is known, and apply kVA ratings to estimate allowable currents.

Key Points

  1. Example 1: Apparent Power in Single-Phase AC Circuit

    • Voltage ( V = 10 \angle 60^\circ ) V
    • Current ( I = 10 \angle 15^\circ ) A
    • Apparent power ( S = V \times I = 100 ) VA

  2. Example 2: Power Calculation with Complex Impedance

    • Impedance ( Z = 8 + j6 ) Ω (8 Ω resistor in series with inductive reactance 6 Ω)
    • Current ( I = 10 ) A
    • Real power ( P = I^2 R = 800 ) W
    • Reactive power ( Q = I^2 X_L = 600 ) VAR
    • Apparent power ( S = I^2 |Z| = 1000 ) VA

  3. Example 3: Transformer Maximum Input and Output Current

    • Rated apparent power ( S = 10,\text{kVA} )
    • Input voltage ( V_{in} = 2000,\text{V} ), output voltage ( V_{out} = 200,\text{V} )
    • Maximum input current ( I_{in} = \dfrac{S}{V_{in}} = 5,\text{A} )
    • Maximum output current ( I_{out} = \dfrac{S}{V_{out}} = 50,\text{A} )

High School Basic Electricity_AC Power_Apparent Power_PART A Apparent Power_Prof. Chen Cheng-Hsu

This article explains the concepts of apparent power, average power, and reactive power in AC circuits, including their formulas, physical meanings, and real-life analogies.

Key Points (English)

  • Apparent Power (S): The product of voltage (V) and current (I), representing the total power in the circuit. Unit: volt-ampere (VA).
  • Average Power (P): The real power consumed by a pure resistive load, analogous to rent—actual energy spent.
  • Reactive Power (Q): The power stored and released by inductive and capacitive loads, analogous to a deposit—eventually returned.
  • Meaning of Apparent Power:
    • At the source: Total power delivered to the load.
    • At the load: Rated capacity and rated current of electrical equipment.
  • Apparent Power Formula:
    $$
    S = VI = I^2 Z = \frac{V^2}{Z}
    $$
    Unit: volt-ampere (VA).
  • English Terms:
    • Apparent Power: 視在功率
    • Average Power (or Real Power): 平均功率
    • Reactive Power: 虛功率
  • Real-life Analogy:
    • Rent (Average Power): Actual expense.
    • Deposit (Reactive Power): Temporarily paid, eventually returned.
    • Apparent Power: The sum of rent and deposit, representing the total amount you need to prepare.

    • 負載端：表示電力設備的額定容量與額定電流。

    $$
    S = VI = I^2 Z = \frac{V^2}{Z}
    $$

    • 視在功率：Apparent Power
    • 平均功率：Average Power (或 Real Power)
    • 虛功率：Reactive Power
  • 生活化比喻：
    • 租金（平均功率）是實際花費。
    • 押金（虛功率）是暫時繳納，最後會退還。

High School Basic Electricity_AC Power_Average Power_PART A Definition and Example of Average Power Calculation_Prof. Chen Cheng-Hsu

Posted on 2025-08-03 In 基本電學 Views: Disqus:

High School Basic Electricity_AC Power_Average Power_PART A Definition and Example of Average Power Calculation_Prof. Chen Cheng-Hsu

This article explains the definition of average power in AC circuits, the calculation formula, the effect of phase difference, and provides a detailed example.

Key Points

  • Definition of Average Power: The average power is the mean value of the instantaneous power ( p(t) ) over a complete cycle.
  • Voltage and Current Waveforms:
    • Voltage ( v(t) = V_m \sin(\omega t) ) with phase angle set to 0° for simplicity.
    • Current ( i(t) = I_m \sin(\omega t - \theta) ).
  • Instantaneous Power Formula:
    $$
    p(t) = v(t) \times i(t) = VI \cos \theta - VI \cos(2\omega t - \theta)
    $$
  • Average Power Calculation:
    • ( VI \cos \theta ) is a constant, product of RMS values and cosine of phase angle.
    • ( VI \cos(2\omega t - \theta) ) is a symmetric sinusoid with zero average.
    • Therefore, average power ( P = VI \cos \theta ).
  • Effect of Phase Difference: If voltage and current have phase angles ( \theta_v ) and ( \theta_i ), then
    $$
    P = VI \cos(\theta_v - \theta_i)
    $$
  • Example Calculation:
    • Voltage: ( v(t) = 50\sqrt{2} \sin(377t + 30^\circ) ), RMS ( V = 50 ), phase angle ( \theta_v = 30^\circ ).
    • Current: ( i(t) = 5\sqrt{2} \sin(377t - 30^\circ) ), RMS ( I = 5 ), phase angle ( \theta_i = -30^\circ ).
    • Average power:
      $$
      P = 50 \times 5 \times \cos(30^\circ - (-30^\circ)) = 250 \times \cos 60^\circ = 125 \text{W}
      $$

本文說明交流電平均功率的定義、計算公式、相位差影響，並提供詳細範例。

重點歸納

  • 平均功率定義：平均功率是完整週期內瞬間功率 ( p(t) ) 的平均值。
  • 電壓與電流波形：
    • 電壓 ( v(t) = V_m \sin(\omega t) )，相位角設為0度方便說明。
    • 電流 ( i(t) = I_m \sin(\omega t - \theta) )。
  • 瞬間功率公式：
    $$
    p(t) = v(t) \times i(t) = VI \cos \theta - VI \cos(2\omega t - \theta)
    $$
  • 平均功率計算：
    • ( VI \cos \theta ) 是常數，為有效值乘上相位角的餘弦。
    • ( VI \cos(2\omega t - \theta) ) 是正負對稱的正弦波，平均值為0。
    • 因此平均功率 ( P = VI \cos \theta )。
  • 相位差影響：若電壓與電流相位角分別為 ( \theta_v ) 與 ( \theta_i )，則
    $$
    P = VI \cos(\theta_v - \theta_i)
    $$

    • 電流：( i(t) = 5\sqrt{2} \sin(377t - 30^\circ) )，有效值 ( I = 5 )，相位角 ( \theta_i = -30^\circ )。
    • 平均功率：
      $$
      P = 50 \times 5 \times \cos(30^\circ - (-30^\circ)) = 250 \times \cos 60^\circ = 125 \text{瓦}
      $$

Power Characteristics of Pure Capacitive AC Circuit

This article summarizes the power characteristics of pure capacitive AC circuits, including phase difference, average power, reactive power, and waveform properties.

Key Points Summary (English)

  1. Voltage and current in a pure capacitive AC circuit have a phase difference of -90 degrees, with current leading voltage by 90 degrees.
  2. Average power is zero because positive and negative power waves cancel out; pure capacitors do not consume average power.
  3. Reactive power (Q_c) formulas:
    • (Q_c = VI = I^2 X_c = \frac{V^2}{X_c})
  4. Instantaneous maximum power (P_{max} = VI) and minimum power (P_{min} = -VI)**.
  5. Power frequency is twice the source frequency.
  6. Power waveforms of pure inductors and capacitors differ by 180 degrees, being opposite in phase.
  7. When inductors and capacitors coexist in a circuit, their reactive powers subtract.

Advanced Example Analysis of RL Discharge Transient Circuit

Overview

This comprehensive article provides an advanced example analysis of RL discharge transient circuits. We examine complex circuit configurations, apply superposition theorem, and demonstrate sophisticated problem-solving techniques for RL discharge phenomena.

Advanced RL Discharge Circuit Analysis

Circuit Configuration

Complex RL Discharge Circuit:

  • Inductor: L = 2 H (energy storage element)
  • Resistors: Multiple resistors in series-parallel configuration
  • Switch: Controls transition from steady state to discharge
  • Power Supply: Provides initial energy for charging
  • Multiple Paths: Complex discharge path analysis

Steady State Analysis

Before Switch Closure:

  • Inductor State: Fully charged, behaves as short circuit
  • Voltage Distribution:
    • Inductor voltage: 0V (steady state)
    • Resistor voltages: Distributed according to voltage division
  • Current: Calculated using total voltage and resistance
  • Energy State: Maximum magnetic energy stored

Current Calculation:
$$ I_{steady} = \frac{V_{total}}{R_{total}} = \frac{100V}{5k\Omega} = 20mA $$

Transient Analysis

1. Initial Discharge Conditions

When Switch S Closes:

  • Circuit Reconfiguration: New discharge path established
  • Inductor Behavior: Begins discharging stored energy
  • Initial Current: Maintains pre-closure value (20mA)
  • Voltage Polarity: Reverses compared to charging state

2. Voltage Analysis

Inductor Voltage Calculation:
$$ V_L(0) = I(0) \times R_{discharge} = 20mA \times 4k\Omega = 80V $$

Voltage Polarity:

  • Charging: Positive voltage across inductor
  • Discharging: Negative voltage across inductor (opposite polarity)

3. Time Constant Analysis

Discharge Time Constant:
$$ \tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{2H}{4k\Omega} = 0.5ms $$

Physical Interpretation:

  • Fast Discharge: Small time constant indicates rapid energy release
  • High Resistance: Large resistance in discharge path
  • Energy Dissipation: Rapid power transfer to resistors

Mathematical Analysis

1. Current Decay Formula

Exponential Decay Expression:
$$ i(t) = I_0 e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = 20mA \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

Formula Components:

  • ( i(t) )：Current at time t
  • ( I_0 )：Initial current (20mA)
  • ( t )：Time after discharge begins
  • ( τ )：Time constant (0.5ms)

2. Voltage Decay Formula

Inductor Voltage:
$$ V_L(t) = V_L(0) e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = 80V \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

Resistor Voltage:
$$ V_R(t) = i(t) \times R = 20mA \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} \times 4k\Omega = 80V \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

3. Time-Dependent Calculations

At t = 1ms (2τ):
$$ i(1ms) = 20mA \cdot e^{-2} = 20mA \cdot 0.135 = 2.7mA $$

$$ V_L(1ms) = 80V \cdot e^{-2} = 80V \cdot 0.135 = 10.8V $$

Superposition Theorem Application

1. Circuit Decomposition

Original Circuit: Complex RL discharge circuit
Decomposed Circuits:

  • Circuit A: Steady-state current source
  • Circuit B: Discharging inductor

2. Individual Circuit Analysis

Circuit A (Steady State):

  • Current Source: 20mA flowing through 4kΩ resistor
  • Voltage: ( V_A = 20mA \times 4k\Omega = 80V )

Circuit B (Discharge):

  • Inductor Current: Decaying from 20mA
  • Voltage: ( V_B(t) = 80V \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} )

3. Superposition Result

Switch S Current:
$$ I_S(t) = I_{steady} + i_{discharge}(t) = 20mA + 20mA \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

At t = 1ms:
$$ I_S(1ms) = 20mA + 2.7mA = 22.7mA $$

Total Current Through Switch:
$$ I_{total} = 100mA - 2.7mA = 97.3mA $$

Energy Considerations

1. Initial Magnetic Energy

Stored Energy:
$$ W_L = \frac{1}{2} L I_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 2H \times (20mA)^2 = 0.4mJ $$

2. Power Dissipation

Instantaneous Power:
$$ P_R(t) = i^2(t) R = (20mA)^2 \times 4k\Omega \times e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau}} = 1.6W \times e^{-\frac{2t}{0.5ms}} $$

3. Energy Transfer

Total Energy Dissipated:
$$ W_{total} = \int_0^{\infty} P_R(t) dt = 0.4mJ $$

Verification:
$$ W_{total} = \frac{1}{2} L I_0^2 = 0.4mJ $$

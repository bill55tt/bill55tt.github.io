Calculation of Parallel Capacitor Size and Power Factor Improvement

Introduction

  • Scope: Derive required capacitor reactive power for PF correction and convert it to capacitance.
  • Why it matters: Proper capacitor sizing reduces current, losses, and penalties while improving voltage regulation.
  • What you’ll learn: Use PF angles to compute $Q_C$ and convert to $C$ via frequency and voltage.

Key Points

  • PF improvement by adding a shunt capacitor across an inductive load.
  • Reactive power:
    • Before: $ Q_L = P\tan\theta_1 $
    • After: $ Q_T = P\tan\theta_2 $
    • Capacitor: $ Q_C = Q_L - Q_T = P(\tan\theta_1 - \tan\theta_2) $
  • Capacitance sizing:

$$C = \frac{Q_C}{2\pi f V^2}$$
where $f$ is frequency, $V$ is voltage (RMS), $Q_C$ is capacitor reactive power.

English Version

Title

Apparent Power Examples (Part B)

Introduction

  • Scope: Walk through three typical scenarios of apparent power S in AC circuits: phasor product, complex impedance, and transformer ratings.
  • Why it matters: Apparent power bridges voltage and current magnitudes regardless of phase, underpinning sizing of equipment and conductors.
  • What you’ll learn: How to compute S, separate it into P and Q when impedance is known, and apply kVA ratings to estimate allowable currents.

Key Points

  1. Example 1: Apparent Power in Single-Phase AC Circuit

    • Voltage $ V = 10 \angle 60^\circ $ V
    • Current $ I = 10 \angle 15^\circ $ A
    • Apparent power $ S = V \times I = 100 $ VA

  2. Example 2: Power Calculation with Complex Impedance

    • Impedance $ Z = 8 + j6 $ Ω (8 Ω resistor in series with inductive reactance 6 Ω)
    • Current $ I = 10 $ A
    • Real power $ P = I^2 R = 800 $ W
    • Reactive power $ Q = I^2 X_L = 600 $ VAR
    • Apparent power $ S = I^2 |Z| = 1000 $ VA

  3. Example 3: Transformer Maximum Input and Output Current

    • Rated apparent power $ S = 10,\text{kVA} $
    • Input voltage $ V_{in} = 2000,\text{V} $, output voltage $ V_{out} = 200,\text{V} $
    • Maximum input current $ I_{in} = \frac{S}{V_{in}} = 5,\text{A} $
    • Maximum output current $ I_{out} = \frac{S}{V_{out}} = 50,\text{A} $

串聯電路功率因數 (PF) 與並聯電路功率因數的關係 / Relationship Between PF of Series and Parallel Circuits

Posted on 2025-08-22 Edited on 2026-03-01 In AC Circuits Views: Disqus:

English Version

Title

Relationship Between Power Factor (PF) of Series and Parallel Circuits

Introduction

  • Scope: Show closed-form PF formulas for RL series and parallel and their relation.
  • Why it matters: Lets you translate PF between topologies instantly when $R$ and $X_L$ match.
  • What you’ll learn: Series PF, parallel PF, and identity $PFS^2 + PFP^2 = 1$.

Key Points

  • Series PF $PFS$:

$$PFS = \frac{R}{\sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2}}$$

  • Parallel PF $PFP$:

$$PFP = \frac{X_L}{\sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2}}$$

  • If $R$ and $X_L$ are identical in both circuits:

$$PFS^2 + PFP^2 = 1$$

  • Example: $R=4,\Omega, X_L=3,\Omega$ → $PFS=0.8$, $PFP=0.6$

高中基本電學_交流電功率_題型二：PT/QT/ST 與 PF 的計算步驟_PART A（延伸）

English Version

Title

Basic Electrical Engineering – AC Power – Problem Type 2: Steps for PT/QT/ST and PF (PART A, Extended)

Introduction

  • Scope: Solve a representative circuit (type 2) by computing branch currents, then PT, QT, ST, and PF.
  • Why it matters: A structured flow avoids mixing P and Q contributions and clarifies lagging vs. leading PF.
  • What you’ll learn: How to apply current division, sum active/reactive power, and derive overall apparent power and PF.

Key Points Summary

  1. Example Problem Introduction
    • Circuit type 2 for PT, QT, ST, and PF
    • Tasks: compute powers and power factor
  2. Calculation of Branch Currents
    • Use current division to obtain RMS currents $ I_1, I_2, I_3 $
    • $ I_1, I_2 $ via impedance division; $ I_3 $ equals total current
  3. Total Active Power $P_T$
    • Resistor power: $ P = I^2 R $
    • Sum power on 5 Ω and 1 Ω resistors
  4. Total Reactive Power $Q_T$
    • Reactors/capacitor: $ Q = I^2 X $
    • Sum inductive Q and subtract capacitive Q
  5. Total Apparent Power $S_T$
    • $ S_T = \sqrt{P_T^2 + Q_T^2} $
  6. Power Factor $PF$
    • $ PF = \frac{P_T}{S_T} $, indicate lagging (inductive) or leading (capacitive)

  2. 各支路電流計算

  3. 總平均功率 $P_T$ 計算

串聯電路功率因數 (PF) 與並聯電路功率因數的關係

Posted on 2025-08-21 Edited on 2026-03-01 In AC Circuits Views: Disqus:

English Version

Title

Relationship Between Power Factor (PF) of Series and Parallel Circuits

Introduction

  • Scope: Derive and compare power factor definitions for series RL and parallel RL circuits with the same R and XL.
  • Why it matters: Understanding PF relationships helps convert results between topologies and quickly estimate PF without re-deriving.
  • What you’ll learn: Closed-form PF formulas, the identity $PFS^2 + PFP^2 = 1$, and a numerical example.

Key Points

  • Power Factor of Series Circuit (PFS):

$$PFS = \frac{R}{\sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2}}$$

  • Power Factor of Parallel Circuit (PFP):

$$PFP = \frac{X_L}{\sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2}}$$

  • If $R$ and $X_L$ are the same in both circuits:

$$PFS^2 + PFP^2 = 1$$

  • Example:
    • $R = 4,\Omega, ; X_L = 3,\Omega$
    • Series PF $PFS = 0.8$
    • Parallel PF $PFP = 0.6$

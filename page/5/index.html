Title

Methods and Principles for Power Factor Improvement

Introduction

  • Scope: Explain why low PF occurs, how it impacts systems, and practical correction methods.
  • Why it matters: PF affects current, losses, voltage regulation, and equipment sizing; utilities may penalize low PF.
  • What you’ll learn: Power triangle concepts (P/Q/S), capacitor compensation, and modern automatic PF controllers.

Key Points

  • Low PF mainly comes from inductive loads (e.g., motors) producing reactive power $Q_L$.
  • In the power triangle, active power $P$ and reactive power $Q_L$ form apparent power $S$.
  • Power factor: $ PF = \cos\theta = P/S $. Larger $Q$ → larger $\theta$ → lower PF.
  • Improvement method: add a shunt capacitor near the inductive load to generate $Q_C$ that offsets $Q_L$.
  • After compensation: total reactive $ Q_T = Q_L - Q_C $ decreases, $\theta$ shrinks, PF rises.
  • Modern solutions often use computerized PF controllers for automatic, stepwise capacitor switching.

Title

High School Basic Electricity – AC Power – Power Factor – PART B: θ and PF under Different Loads

Introduction

  • Scope: Summarize how phase angle θ and power factor PF behave across resistive, inductive, and capacitive loads.
  • Why it matters: Clarifies leading/lagging conventions and how PF relates to circuit topology.
  • What you’ll learn: Phase relations (V leads I or I leads V) and PF extremes in pure R/L/C and series RL/RC.

Key Points Summary

  1. PF angle ($\theta$) and PF definitions.
  2. Inductive circuits: Voltage leads current (mnemonic “VL” in VLSIC).
  3. Capacitive circuits: Current leads voltage (mnemonic “IC” in VLSIC).
  4. Pure resistive load: in-phase, $\theta = 0^\circ$, PF = 1.
  5. Pure inductive load: current lags by 90°, $\theta = 90^\circ$, PF = 0 (reactive only).
  6. Pure capacitive load: current leads by 90°, $\theta = 90^\circ$, PF = 0 (reactive only).
  7. Inductive load (series RL): 0° < $\theta$ < 90°, 0 < PF < 1 (lagging).
  8. Capacitive load (series RC): 0° < $\theta$ < 90°, 0 < PF < 1 (leading).

High School Basic Electricity – AC Power – Power Triangle and Complex Power – PART B: Complex Power (Summary: 0:12–8:14)

Introduction

  • Scope: Summarize relationships among P, Q, and S; connect the power triangle to complex power.
  • Why it matters: Complex power clarifies sign conventions and the roles of inductive vs. capacitive loads.
  • What you’ll learn: P–Q–S identities, phase angle interpretation, and the definition $ \bar{S} = \bar{V},\bar{I}^* $.

Key Points Summary

  1. Meaning and Use of the Power Triangle
    • Understand relations between active power (P), reactive power (Q), apparent power (S).
    • Map triangle representation to complex power notation.
  2. P, Q, S Relationships
    • $ P = S\cos\theta $
    • $ Q = S\sin\theta $
    • $ S = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2} $
  3. Phase Angle $\theta$
    • $\theta$ is the voltage–current phase difference.
    • RL (inductive): $ \bar{S} = P + jQ $
    • RC (capacitive): $ \bar{S} = P - jQ $
  4. Definition of Complex Power
    • $ \bar{S} = \bar{V},\bar{I}^* $
    • Conjugation yields correct phase difference $\theta = \theta_V - \theta_I$.

Advanced Example Analysis of RL Discharge Transient Circuit

Overview

This comprehensive article provides an advanced example analysis of RL discharge transient circuits. We examine complex circuit configurations, apply superposition theorem, and demonstrate sophisticated problem-solving techniques for RL discharge phenomena.

Advanced RL Discharge Circuit Analysis

Circuit Configuration

Complex RL Discharge Circuit:

  • Inductor: L = 2 H (energy storage element)
  • Resistors: Multiple resistors in series-parallel configuration
  • Switch: Controls transition from steady state to discharge
  • Power Supply: Provides initial energy for charging
  • Multiple Paths: Complex discharge path analysis

Steady State Analysis

Before Switch Closure:

  • Inductor State: Fully charged, behaves as short circuit
  • Voltage Distribution:
    • Inductor voltage: 0V (steady state)
    • Resistor voltages: Distributed according to voltage division
  • Current: Calculated using total voltage and resistance
  • Energy State: Maximum magnetic energy stored

Current Calculation:
$$ W_L = \frac{1}{2} L I_0^2 $$

Transient Analysis

1. Initial Discharge Conditions

When Switch S Closes:

  • Circuit Reconfiguration: New discharge path established
  • Inductor Behavior: Begins discharging stored energy
  • Initial Current: Maintains pre-closure value (20\text{ mA})
  • Voltage Polarity: Reverses compared to charging state

2. Voltage Analysis

Inductor Voltage Calculation:
$$ V_L(0) = I(0) \times R_{discharge} = 20mA \times 4k\Omega = 80V $$

Voltage Polarity:

  • Charging: Positive voltage across inductor
  • Discharging: Negative voltage across inductor (opposite polarity)

3. Time Constant Analysis

Discharge Time Constant:
$$ \tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{2H}{4k\Omega} = 0.5ms $$

Physical Interpretation:

  • Fast Discharge: Small time constant indicates rapid energy release
  • High Resistance: Large resistance in discharge path
  • Energy Dissipation: Rapid power transfer to resistors

Mathematical Analysis

1. Current Decay Formula

Exponential Decay Expression:
$$ i(t) = I_0 e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = 20mA \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

Formula Components:

  • $ i(t) $：Current at time t
  • $ I_0 $：Initial current (20\text{ mA})
  • $ t $：Time after discharge begins
  • $ \tau $：Time constant (0.5\text{ ms})

2. Voltage Decay Formula

Inductor Voltage:
$$ V_L(t) = V_L(0) e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = 80V \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

Resistor Voltage:
$$ V_R(t) = i(t) \times R = 20mA \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} \times 4k\Omega = 80V \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

3. Time-Dependent Calculations

At t = 1ms (2τ):
$$ i(1ms) = 20mA \cdot e^{-2} = 20mA \cdot 0.135 = 2.7mA $$

$$ V_L(1ms) = 80V \cdot e^{-2} = 80V \cdot 0.135 = 10.8V $$

Superposition Theorem Application

1. Circuit Decomposition

Original Circuit: Complex RL discharge circuit
Decomposed Circuits:

  • Circuit A: Steady-state current source
  • Circuit B: Discharging inductor

2. Individual Circuit Analysis

Circuit A (Steady State):

  • Current Source: 20\text{ mA} flowing through 4\text{ k}\Omega resistor
  • Voltage: $ V_A = 20\text{ mA} \times 4k\Omega = 80\text{ V} $

Circuit B (Discharge):

  • Inductor Current: Decaying from 20\text{ mA}
  • Voltage: $ V_B(t) = 80\text{ V} \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5\text{ ms}}} $

3. Superposition Result

Switch S Current:
$$ I_S(t) = I_{steady} + i_{discharge}(t) = 20mA + 20mA \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

At t = 1\text{ ms}:
$$ I_S(1ms) = 20mA + 2.7mA = 22.7mA $$

Total Current Through Switch:
$$ I_{total} = 100mA - 2.7mA = 97.3mA $$

Energy Considerations

1. Initial Magnetic Energy

Stored Energy:
$$ W_L = \frac{1}{2} L I_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 2H \times (20mA)^2 = 0.4mJ $$

2. Power Dissipation

Instantaneous Power:
$$ P_R(t) = i^2(t) R = (20mA)^2 \times 4k\Omega \times e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau}} = 1.6W \times e^{-\frac{2t}{0.5ms}} $$

3. Energy Transfer

Total Energy Dissipated:
$$ W_{total} = \int_0^{\infty} P_R(t) dt = 0.4mJ $$

Verification:
$$ W_{total} = \frac{1}{2} L I_0^2 = 0.4mJ $$

物理解釋：

  • 快速放電：小時間常數表示快速能量釋放
  • 高電阻：放電路徑中的大電阻
  • 能量消耗：快速功率轉移到電阻

數學分析

1. 電流衰減公式

指數衰減表達式：
$$ i(t) = I_0 e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = 20mA \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

公式組成：

  • $ i(t) $：時間 t 時的電流
  • $ I_0 $：初始電流（20\text{ mA}）
  • $ t $：放電開始後的時間
  • $ \tau $：時間常數（0.5\text{ ms}）

2. 電壓衰減公式

電感電壓：
$$ V_L(t) = V_L(0) e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = 80V \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

電阻電壓：
$$ V_R(t) = i(t) \times R = 20mA \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} \times 4k\Omega = 80V \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

3. 時間相關計算

t = 1ms 時（2τ）：
$$ i(1ms) = 20mA \cdot e^{-2} = 20mA \cdot 0.135 = 2.7mA $$

$$ V_L(1ms) = 80V \cdot e^{-2} = 80V \cdot 0.135 = 10.8V $$

重疊定理應用

1. 電路分解

原始電路：複雜 RL 放電電路
分解電路：

  • 電路 A：穩態電流源
  • 電路 B：放電電感

2. 個別電路分析

電路 A（穩態）：

  • 電流源：20mA 流過 4kΩ 電阻
  • 電壓：$ V_A = 20\text{ mA} \times 4k\Omega = 80\text{ V} $

電路 B（放電）：

  • 電感電流：從 20mA 衰減
  • 電壓：$ V_B(t) = 80\text{ V} \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5\text{ ms}}} $

3. 重疊結果

開關 S 電流：
$$ I_S(t) = I_{steady} + i_{discharge}(t) = 20mA + 20mA \cdot e^{-\frac{t}{0.5ms}} $$

t = 1ms 時：
$$ I_S(1ms) = 20mA + 2.7mA = 22.7mA $$

開關總電流：
$$ I_{total} = 100mA - 2.7mA = 97.3mA $$

能量考量

1. 初始磁能

儲存能量：
$$ W_L = \frac{1}{2} L I_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 2H \times (20mA)^2 = 0.4mJ $$

2. 功率消耗

瞬時功率：
$$ P_R(t) = i^2(t) R = (20mA)^2 \times 4k\Omega \times e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau}} = 1.6W \times e^{-\frac{2t}{0.5ms}} $$

3. 能量轉移

總能量消耗：
$$ W_{total} = \int_0^{\infty} P_R(t) dt = 0.4mJ $$

驗證：
$$ W_{total} = \frac{1}{2} L I_0^2 = 0.4mJ $$

  • 混合配置：組合效應

進階數學技巧

1. 微分方程式

一階 RL 電路：
$$ L \frac{di(t)}{dt} + Ri(t) = 0 $$

求解方法：
$$ i(t) = I_0 e^{-\frac{R}{L}t} = I_0 e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} $$

2. 拉普拉斯變換

轉移函數：
$$ H(s) = \frac{I(s)}{V(s)} = \frac{1}{Ls + R} $$

極點分析：
$$ s = -\frac{R}{L} = -\frac{1}{\tau} $$

3. 狀態空間分析

狀態變數：
$$ x(t) = i(t) $$

狀態方程式：
$$ \dot{x}(t) = -\frac{R}{L}x(t) $$
